# Where the four futures sit — Dator's visioning process

Dator (2009, pp. 2–3; provenance rows 33–35) describes the four futures
exercise as one part of a larger process, run in this order and without
skipping steps. Summarised in our words:

1. **Appreciating the past** — a shared understanding of the community's or organisation's history, back to its beginning if possible.
2. **Understanding the present** — airing problems and satisfactions. Until people have done this, they tend to resist thinking about the future.
3. **Forecasting aspects of the futures** — trends, emerging issues and continuities over a roughly 20–50-year horizon.
4. **Experiencing alternative futures** — the four generic futures. Dator calls this the most crucial component.
5. **Envisioning the futures** — a preferred future, created only after the alternatives have been experienced.
6. **Creating the futures** — deciding what to do now, and in what order, to move toward the preferred future.
7. **Institutionalising futures research** — an ongoing scanning function, and a plan to repeat the process.

`fdbx-four-futures` covers component 4 in depth. Step 1 of the skill touches
components 1–3 briefly, and Step 5 opens component 5.

> **fdbx adaptation:** in design work, components 1–3 are often already partly
> done in discovery research. Bring those findings in as `user-supplied` rows in
> the Evidence Ledger rather than regenerating them.
