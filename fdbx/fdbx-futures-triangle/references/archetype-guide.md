# Archetype guide — the five images in design work

The five archetypes and their core beliefs come from Inayatullah (2008,
pp. 7–8; provenance rows 6–10). The "in product and service work" column and
the tells are fdbx's, offered as prompts, not as part of the source.

| Archetype | Core belief (source) | In product and service work (fdbx) | Tell-tale language |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Evolution and progress** | More technology and rationality lead forward | AI-first roadmaps, automation, "smart" everything, growth as default | "next-generation", "frictionless", "scale" |
| **Collapse** | Limits reached or overshot; things worsen | Resilience, offline modes, survival of the business model, risk registers | "if the platform goes down", "when funding dries up" |
| **Gaia** | Repair and inclusion; partnership between people, nature, technology | Circular services, community ownership, care-centred design, regenerative models | "stewardship", "for everyone", "give back" |
| **Globalism** | Barriers fall; integration brings shared wealth | Global platforms, interoperability, one product for all markets | "borderless", "one standard", "global reach" |
| **Back to the future** | Return to a simpler, clearer, ordered past | Analogue revivals, digital minimalism, local and heritage services, "like it used to be" | "simpler", "human", "the way it was" |

## Using the guide

- Search the brief, the roadmap and stakeholder interviews for tell-tale language. It often shows which image is doing the pulling.
- A single organisation usually holds more than one image: leadership in *progress*, frontline staff in *back to the future*, the sustainability team in *Gaia*. Map each with its holder.
- If an image fits none of the five, name it and describe its core belief. Inayatullah says "five or so": the set is open.
