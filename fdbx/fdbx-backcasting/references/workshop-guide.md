# Running a backcast with a group

The GO-Science *Futures Toolkit* (pp. 92–96) runs backcasting as a workshop.
This guide summarises its setup and adds notes for design teams, marked as
fdbx adaptations.

## Who and how long

- **8–16 people**, split into smaller groups if you have enough facilitators.
- **4–5 hours over two workshops**, plus time to prepare and to write up the action plan.
- Invite the people who hold the policy or product area, and think about the other stakeholders needed to make the preferred future happen. Events scored "wholly out of our control" are easier to discuss when someone who does control them is in the room.

## Suggested split across two sessions

| Session | Steps | Toolkit timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduce the preferred future; key differences; build the timeline | 30 + 60 + 40–50 minutes |
| 2 | Score control; events in control; events outside control; action plan | 40 + 40 + 30 minutes, then the plan |

## The quick version (about 90 minutes)

1. Split into two groups. One builds a timeline forward from the present to the preferred future; the other builds it backward from the future to today.
2. The groups do not look at each other's wall until the time is up. About half an hour is enough.
3. Put the two timelines side by side. Discuss the differences and similarities; if there is time, ask some of the Step 5 questions.
4. Blend them into one timeline. The places where they must meet are the key decision points.

The toolkit says this version brings energy to a group that is new to futures thinking.

## Notes for design teams

> **fdbx adaptation.**

- Open with the memories exercise: each person writes one first-person memory from the target year ("Looking back, the turning point was…") before any timeline is drawn. It gets people speaking from inside the future.
- Keep a visible "unknowns" sheet. When the group needs a present-day figure nobody has, it goes on the sheet with an owner, not into the timeline as a guess.
- Give the "who loses out" column its own time. It is the question groups most often skip.
