# Horizon perspectives

Sharpe et al. (2016, Table 2, PDF p. 10; provenance row 28) record how people
in each horizon see the others, first as a fixed, negative mindset and then as
a positive perspective. They report it as one of the most useful things
participants take from a session. Below is our paraphrase of the pattern, not
a reproduction of the table. Use it as a prompt and write the views in the
user's own context.

| Looking from | At | Fixed mindset (paraphrase) | Positive perspective (paraphrase) |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | H1 | A rival to beat | Shared infrastructure; allies with common interests |
| H1 | H2 | A drain on investment, to be watched | A source of ideas and improvement |
| H1 | H3 | Irrelevant fantasy, or a threat to suppress | Hope and possible renewal |
| H2 | H1 | Slow and in the way | Where innovation lands and scales; a source of support |
| H2 | H2 | Competitors for resources | Allies building momentum |
| H2 | H3 | Impractical | Inspiration and direction |
| H3 | H1 | The problem itself | Skills, assets and heritage worth redeploying and protecting |
| H3 | H2 | Compromisers diluting the vision | Stepping stones; allies |
| H3 | H3 | Rival visions | A debate that brings deeper values into play |

## Using it

- Write each row for the actual people involved: the operations director, the innovation lab, the community group.
- The shift the practice aims for is from *holding* a horizon as a fixed mindset to *using* all three as perspectives. Sharpe et al. call the ability to hold all three "future consciousness".
- When a stakeholder is stereotyped as purely H1 or purely H3, look for where they already hold another horizon's view. Sharpe et al. report that surfacing this dissolved disagreements in the Carnegie case.
