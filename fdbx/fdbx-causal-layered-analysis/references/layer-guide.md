# Layer guide — probing questions

Questions to ask at each layer. The toolbox questions under the worldview layer
paraphrase Inayatullah's five poststructural concepts (1998, § The
poststructural futures toolbox; provenance rows 22–26). The other prompts are
fdbx's.

## Litany

- How is this issue described in the news, in the brief, in the board deck?
- What number or metric stands for the problem?
- Who is blamed, and who is expected to fix it?
- What is the emotional tone: fear, helplessness, impatience?

## Social causes

- What economic, political, technological, cultural and historical factors produce the litany?
- Which of these does the organisation already measure, and which does it not?
- What systemic fix does this layer suggest, and which institutions would have to cooperate?
- Which causes are supported by evidence the user supplied, and which are model assumptions? (Evidence Ledger.)

## Discourse / worldview — the five toolbox questions

1. **Deconstruction.** Who is privileged by the current framing, and who is silenced? Which future does it quietly assume is preferable?
2. **Genealogy.** Which ways of talking about this issue won out historically? When did it become an issue at all?
3. **Distance.** What scenario would make the present look strange or unfamiliar? A far place, a utopia, a future that could have happened?
4. **Alternative pasts and futures.** Whose version of the past is treated as true? Which vision of the future keeps the present in place?
5. **Reordering knowledge.** How would this issue be ordered differently across cultures, genders, or ways of knowing? What, or who, is treated as "other"?

Then name at least two discourses and, for each, the problem it constitutes.

## Myth / metaphor

- What proverb, image, or story sums up how people feel about this issue?
- If the issue were a place, an animal, or a journey, what would it be?
- What metaphor would a child, an elder, or someone from another culture use?
- What alternative metaphor would make a different future feel natural?

## Moving back up

- If the alternative metaphor were true, what worldview would follow?
- What would institutions do differently under that worldview?
- How would the headline, and the brief, read then?
