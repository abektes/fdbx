# Horizon Scanning

**The short version:** Collect signals of change from real sources, one short sourced record per signal, and turn them into a handful of drivers worth acting on. Start with a question, check you're not only looking at technology, and let the themes come from the signals.

---

## Who made it

Horizon scanning is standard practice in government foresight. This skill follows the UK Government Office for Science *Futures Toolkit* (2nd edition, 2024), which defines it as the systematic collection of insights on emerging trends and weak signals of change, and sets it out in five steps.

---

## The problem it solves

Every futures method rests on claims about the present: what is changing, and how fast. Teams usually take these from memory, from the last conference they attended, or now from an AI model, which states them fluently with no source. Asked for a scan, a plain model lists trends as present-day fact without a single source. Given signals from a team, it can add figures they never supplied.

Horizon scanning makes the present an input you can check. Every signal has a source, a date and a reason it matters.

---

## When to use it

- Before setting strategy, to see what is changing around your product or service
- When staff have collected signals and you want to know what they add up to
- When another futures method needs real inputs: pushes for the Futures Triangle, pockets of the future for Three Horizons, events outside your control for Backcasting
- When you want a scanning routine, not a one-off report

---

## What it produces

- **A scoping question** that decides what counts as a signal
- **Scans**: one row per signal, with its source, date, evidence type, PESTLE category and why it matters
- **A balance check**: how many scans fall in each category, and the gaps
- **A natural agenda**: themes that emerge from the scans themselves
- **Signal types**: established trend, expected development, emerging issue, risk or opportunity, possible event
- **Impact, likelihood and newness** ratings for each theme
- **Four to eight drivers of change** to take forward, with recommendations or research needs
- **Leads to verify**: things the AI thinks may be relevant but could not source, kept out of the analysis

---

## The key insight

A scan is only as good as its sources, so the toolkit's small rules carry the method: go back to the original source, include the link, count categories so technology doesn't crowd out everything else, and include signals that seem irrelevant at first.

For AI, fdbx adds one more rule: **a model's memory is not a source.** The skill takes signals only from you or from a search it runs with links. Anything else goes on a separate list to check.

---

## How to use it with AI

Give a topic or scoping question and a horizon. Then either paste your team's signals (*analyse* mode), let the skill search if your assistant has web search (*scan* mode), or ask for a scanning plan to run yourselves (*plan* mode). The output ends with an Evidence Ledger and a suggested next method.
