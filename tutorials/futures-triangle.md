# Futures Triangle

**The short version:** Map the future of an issue as three forces: the images of the future that **pull**, the trends that **push**, and the barriers that **weigh** each image down. Then see which future is plausible, and which one your strategy is quietly serving.

---

## Who made it

Sohail Inayatullah describes the Futures Triangle in "Six Pillars: futures thinking for transforming" (*Foresight*, 2008) as a tool of the first pillar, *Mapping*. It sits alongside "shared history" (a timeline of how the present came about) and the "futures landscape".

---

## The problem it solves

Roadmaps are full of sentences like "the market is moving toward…". They rarely say *who* believes that, *what* is actually pushing in that direction, or *what* stands in the way. Different people in the same organisation often hold different images of the future without knowing it.

The triangle puts these side by side. It also catches the case where a team is designing for a future it never chose, which Inayatullah calls a **used future**: an image of the future borrowed, unconsciously, from someone else.

---

## When to use it

- A roadmap assumes a direction without showing why
- The team needs a shared picture of what is driving change in its sector
- Stakeholders hold different visions and nobody has compared them
- Before building scenarios, so they rest on actual forces rather than preferences
- You suspect the product is built for someone else's future

---

## What it produces

- **Pull:** at least three images of the future, each with who holds it, mapped to Inayatullah's archetypes: *evolution and progress*, *collapse*, *Gaia*, *globalism*, *back to the future*. New images are allowed.
- **Push:** drivers and trends, each typed in an Evidence Ledger as user-supplied, sourced, or assumption.
- **Weight:** the specific barriers holding back each image. They differ from image to image.
- **Interaction:** the plausible future, the contested images (pulling hard but weighed down), and the *unchosen pushes* (trends heading somewhere nobody wants).
- **Scenarios:** three or four, each built on an image or a driver.
- **Design implications:** which image your current strategy serves, whether it was chosen or borrowed, and which future the organisation is pushing away.

---

## The key insight

Most futures conversations only talk about pushes (trends) or only about pulls (visions). The triangle's power is in the **weights**, and in seeing that each image has different ones. The same regulation that blocks one future may speed up another. Knowing what weighs on each image tells you where design can actually move things.

---

## How to use it with AI

Give the issue, a horizon year, whose views matter, and any trends or data you already have. The skill maps images with their holders, lists pushes with their evidence type, gives weights per image, analyses the interaction, writes three or four scenarios, and says which future your current strategy is serving. It ends with an Evidence Ledger and a suggested next method.
